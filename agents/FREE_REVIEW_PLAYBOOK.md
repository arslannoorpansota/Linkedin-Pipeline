# The free 30 minute review: what to run, and what to build after

The InMail promises a free 30 minute review. This is what that call actually is,
so it can be delivered the same way every time instead of improvised.

The review is a **diagnostic, not a pitch**. You are finding out what breaks when
the business grows. The proposal writes itself once you know that.

---

## Before the call (10 minutes, on your own)

Everything here is already in the sheet or on their public site.

1. **Open their site** (column S has the URL and platform).
2. **Click through as a customer would.** Book something. Fill the contact form.
   Try to log in. Note where it stops being self serve and a human has to take over.
3. **Count the handoffs.** Every point where their site hands off to email, a phone
   call, a PDF or a spreadsheet is a candidate for the build.
4. **Check for a login.** Column S notes whether we saw one. No login means every
   client interaction is manual.

Write down three specific things you noticed. That is the whole opening.

---

## The call (30 minutes)

### Minutes 0-5: what they actually do

Do not assume the site is current. Ask:

- "Walk me through what happens from someone finding you to becoming a client."
- "Which parts of that do you or your team do by hand right now?"

Listen for repeated manual work. That is the build.

### Minutes 5-20: find the ceiling

The point is to find what breaks at 3x volume. Ask about the busiest process:

- "How many of these do you handle a week? What happens at three times that?"
- "Where does the information live? One system, or several?"
- "What do you re-key from one place into another?"
- "What goes wrong most often, and who notices first?"

Follow the pain, not the feature list. A founder who says "I spend Sunday nights
doing X" has told you exactly what to build.

### Minutes 20-27: play it back

Say what you heard, in their words, as a sequence:

> "So a client books by email, you check the calendar by hand, you send a form,
> they send it back as a photo, and you type it into the record. Four handoffs,
> and the only copy of the truth is your inbox."

If that lands, you have the scope. If they correct you, even better.

### Minutes 27-30: one next step

Offer to send back a short written version of what you heard, with a rough shape
and range. Never quote a number on the call.

## What you are usually selling

Across this list of 93, the same four builds come up. Map what you heard to one.

### 1. The booking and records layer
**Signs:** no login on the site, bookings by email or phone, records in a
spreadsheet or a paper file.
**Build:** client accounts, online booking against real availability, records in
one place, reminders that send themselves.
**Typical:** 4 to 8 weeks.

### 2. The intake layer (clinical)
**Signs:** intake forms as PDFs, notes in Word, HIPAA mentioned nervously.
**Build:** digital intake that feeds the record, structured notes, a compliant
store, role based access.
**Typical:** 6 to 10 weeks. Say plainly that compliance is designed in, not added.

### 3. The ordering layer (device, supplement, lab)
**Signs:** distributors order by email, price lists as PDFs, no reorder history.
**Build:** account login per distributor, catalogue with their pricing, reorder
from history, order status without a phone call.
**Typical:** 6 to 10 weeks.

### 4. The results layer (diagnostics, labs)
**Signs:** results sent as PDF attachments, a spreadsheet tracking who was told.
**Build:** results delivery patients can log in to, status tracking, clinician
view, an audit trail.
**Typical:** 8 to 12 weeks.

## The honest answers to hard questions

**"What will it cost?"**
Not on the first call. "I want to send you a shape first. If the range does not
work, you have lost nothing."

**"Can you show me something similar?"**
Only reference work you can actually name. Do not invent a case study.

**"Why you and not an agency?"**
You are a working CTO, not an account manager. The person who scopes it is the
person who builds it.

**"Do you know our regulations?"**
Say what you know and what you would check. For HIPAA: you build to it, and you
would confirm their specific obligations with their compliance contact before
scoping. Never bluff a regulatory answer.

**"Can you just fix our website?"**
Usually no, and say so. A site on Wix that only needs new copy is not this work.
The build starts where the site needs accounts, data or automation.

---

## When there is nothing to build

Some calls will end with the honest answer that they do not need you yet. Say it.
A founder told "you do not need this for another year" remembers that, and the
review cost you 30 minutes. Trying to manufacture a project from a business that
does not have one costs more than the call.
