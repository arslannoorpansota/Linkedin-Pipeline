## 3. The one insight to walk in with

**His moat and his bottleneck are the same thing:** two independent human reviewers plus adjudication, on every single submission, inside 72 hours.

That is what makes TheSignal credible, and it is also what stops him getting from a handful of pilot employers to the $24k/yr program tier and enterprise. At $2,999 a decision, every hour of reviewer recruiting, calibration, disagreement resolution and report writing comes straight out of margin, and none of it compounds unless there is real tooling underneath it.

**So what we sell is not "add AI to your scoring."** It is: build the system that makes human review fast, consistent, auditable and repeatable, so the standard survives volume.

Say it roughly like this, once, and only after he has described the problem in his own words:

> "The thing that makes TheSignal credible is the part that's hardest to scale. Most people in your position eventually try to fix that by letting a model do the scoring, and that's exactly what would break the product. The other route is to build the tooling around the humans, so each review takes half the time and consistency becomes something you can actually measure. That second one is what my team does."

> ### Hard rule for this call
> **Do not propose AI scoring of candidate work.** It contradicts his published standard, his ethics page and his entire differentiation. AI is welcome anywhere that is *not* the judgment: scenario generation, reviewer prep, draft summarization, evidence extraction, anomaly and plagiarism flags, ops.
