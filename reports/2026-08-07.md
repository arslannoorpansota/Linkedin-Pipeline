- **Agent:** Ops
- **Outcome:** Arslan reported ~111 emails from `noreply-dmarc-support@google.com` ("Report domain: electrocomit.com") and asked how to stop them. Ran a live DNS audit of `electrocomit.com`:
  - **Cause identified — not a security incident.** The published record is `_dmarc.electrocomit.com TXT "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:arslan@electrocomit.com"`. The `rua=` tag explicitly requests aggregate reports be emailed to arslan@. Google is complying. Other providers (Yahoo/Microsoft) will send their own.
  - **Full auth posture:** SPF ✅ `v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all`; DKIM ✅ (`google` selector, 2048-bit, published); MX ✅ Google Workspace; DNS hosted at Namecheap (`dns1/dns2.registrar-servers.com`).
  - **Real gap found: `p=none`.** DMARC is monitor-only, so `arslan@electrocomit.com` is currently spoofable — receiving servers are told to take no action on auth failures. This matters because the entire BD motion sends cold outreach from that address.
  - **Recommended (not yet applied — needs Namecheap access):** repoint `rua=` to a free Postmark DMARC digest address (`v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:<id>@dmarc.postmarkapp.com; fo=1`) to get one weekly readable digest instead of daily XML, then use 2–4 weeks of clean reports to escalate to `p=quarantine` → `p=reject`. Interim: Gmail filter on subject `Report domain:` (not the sender address — multiple providers send these) → Skip Inbox + label "DMARC", then bulk-archive the backlog.
- **Why:** The instinct was to "add security so we stop getting these," but the emails *are* the security feature reporting in. Deleting the DMARC record would be actively harmful — Google/Yahoo have required DMARC for bulk senders since Feb 2024, so removing it would hurt cold-outreach deliverability. Flagged that the reports should be rerouted, never killed.
- **Next action:** Arslan/Faizan to apply the Namecheap TXT change + Gmail filter. Revisit `p=quarantine` once ~4 weeks of digests are clean. No sheet changes.
